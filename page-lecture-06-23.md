Pricing (call) options: The Black–Scholes formula
Perhaps the most remarkable application of the no arbitrage idea is to the pricing of derivative
securities called options.
A call option on an asset is a contract giving one the “option” to buy the asset at a
specified price, called the strike price, at a specified time in the future.
The advantage of a call option is that it allows one to postpone the purchase of an asset
until after one sees the price.
In particular, one can wait for the market price to rise above the strike price, and then
exercise the option. That is, buy at the strike price and resell at the higher market price.
Question: How much is a call option worth?
