## Cones and the course Farkas form

$$
\operatorname{cone}(A)=\{Ax:x\ge0\}
$$

is generated by the columns of $A$ with nonnegative coefficients, unlike a span, which permits either sign.

For $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ and $b\in\mathbb R^m$, exactly one holds:

$$
\exists x\ge0:\ Ax=b;qquad
\exists y\in\mathbb R^m:\ yA\ge0,\quad y\cdot b<0.
$$

The row multiplier $y$ is not automatically nonnegative. Check all certificate inequalities and dimensions. Mutual exclusivity follows from $y\cdot b=yAx\ge0$ if $x\ge0$ and $yA\ge0$.

## Conversions

- $Ax\le b$: add $s\ge0$ to write $Ax+s=b$.
- $Ax\ge b$: subtract $s\ge0$ to write $Ax-s=b$.
- Free $x_j$: write $x_j=u_j-v_j$, where $u_j,v_j\ge0$.

Apply the equality-form lemma to the expanded matrix, then read off the restrictions on $y$. This gives the following alternatives to feasibility:

| Original system | Infeasibility certificate |
|---|---|
| $Ax\le b,\ x\ge0$ | $y\ge0,\ yA\ge0,\ y\cdot b<0$ |
| $Ax\ge b,\ x\ge0$ | $y\ge0,\ yA\le0,\ y\cdot b>0$ |
| $Ax\le b,\ x$ free | $y\ge0,\ yA=0,\ y\cdot b<0$ |

For mixed constraints, derive the signs from the converted columns rather than memorising a mismatched form. Lecture 6 finance-pricing examples illustrate these methods; the revision lecture excludes the finance theories, not the underlying linear algebra.
