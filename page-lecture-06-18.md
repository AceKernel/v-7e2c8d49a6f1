Example (cont’d):
Now apply the Farkas lemma to the system [Bz = b and z ≥0] to get the Farkas
alternative, namely the system [yB ≥0 and y · b < 0].
Since B = [A|I], y[A|I] = yB ≥0, implying that yA ≥0 and yI = y ≥0.
Thus, the Farkas alternative for the system [Ax ≤b and x ≥0] is
[yA ≥0,
y ≥0,
and
y · b < 0].
The punchline here is that by a judicious use of auxiliary variables one can convert almost
anything into the system of equations we started with, namely [Ax = b and x ≥0], and then
apply the Farkas lemma.
